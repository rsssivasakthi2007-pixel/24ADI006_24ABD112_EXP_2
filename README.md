This project presents a comprehensive study on the performance of different activation functions and optimization algorithms used in Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs). The primary objective is to understand how the choice of activation function and optimizer influences the learning process, convergence speed, and overall prediction accuracy of deep learning models. The experiment involves visualizing and comparing the Sigmoid, Tanh, and ReLU activation functions, followed by training identical ANN architectures to evaluate their effectiveness based on training and validation metrics. Additionally, the project compares the performance of popular optimization algorithms, including Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), Momentum, RMSProp, and Adam, by analyzing their loss, accuracy, and convergence behavior. To ensure reproducibility and efficient experiment management, the entire implementation is carried out using Google Colab, with notebooks and trained models stored in Google Drive and version-controlled using GitHub. This project provides practical insights into selecting appropriate activation functions and optimizers while demonstrating the importance of cloud-based development environments and version control in modern deep learning workflows.
